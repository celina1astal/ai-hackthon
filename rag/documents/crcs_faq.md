# CRCS FAQs

Each Q&A below is its own retrieval chunk. Keep them separate — they are
already at the right granularity, which is rare for government content.

## What is a multi-state cooperative society?
A cooperative society whose objects are not confined to one State.

## Which Act governs multi-state cooperative societies?
The Multi-State Cooperative Societies Act, 2002, and the rules made under it.

## Under which section are new MSCS registered?
Section 7 of the MSCS Act, 2002.

## Minimum members to register a new MSCS?
A minimum of 50 individual members from each State must join and sign.

## Format prescribed for the membership list?
Form I, sub-point 5 (annexed after the Schedule to the MSCS Act, 2002).

## How does a society prepare its bye-laws?
Under section 10 of the MSCS Act, 2002. CRCS has prepared sample bye-laws
that may be referred to for guidance.

## Documents required to register a new MSCS

**[A] Base requirements — Form I in the format annexed to the Act, with:**
- Bank certificate stating the credit balance in favour of the proposed society
- A scheme explaining how the society has reasonable prospects of becoming a viable unit
- Four copies of the bye-laws in original
- For credit or multi-purpose societies, the proposed area of operation is initially permitted in two States/UTs only
- A list of at least 50 members from each State in the annexed format, with ID proofs attested by the Chief Promoter
- Certified copies of resolutions of the proposed society and of the promoters, specifying the applicant to whom the Central Registrar may address correspondence
- Contact number and email of the Chief Promoter or society on the cover page

**[B] Additional, for thrift-and-credit and multi-purpose societies:**
- No Objection Certificate from the Registrar of Cooperative Societies of each State/UT in the proposed area of operation
- A certificate that the Chief Promoter's credentials have been verified by the RCS of the State where the head office is proposed

All documents in original, signed by the Chief Promoter on each page.
Already-registered credit/multi-purpose societies expanding into category [B]
must also submit the NOC.

## How long does registration take?
The Central Registrar shall dispose of the application within **four months**
of receipt.

## Under which section is a bye-law amendment applied for?
Section 11 of the MSCS Act.

## Notice period to members for a bye-law amendment?
15 days clear notice.

## Within how many days must the society apply for the amendment?
Within **60 days** of the general meeting at which the amendment was passed.

## How long does an amendment take to be disposed of?
Within **three months** of receipt.

## Is there a fee for registration or for registering a bye-law amendment?
No.

## When does a bye-law amendment come into force?
Unless expressed to come into operation on a particular day, on the day it is
registered.

## What are National Cooperative Societies?
An MSCS specified in the Second Schedule. There are presently 19.

## Maximum number of directors on the board?
21, including reservations: 1 SC/ST member and 2 women members.

## How many directors may be co-opted?
Two.

## Is the Chief Executive elected or appointed?
Appointed by the board.

## What must the bank balance certificate show?
Amounts collected from promoter members deposited in a bank, with the bank
issuing a balance certificate and statement of account **in favour of the
proposed society**.

## What must the viability / project report show?
The society's overall working and economic conditions, effectiveness with
respect to the States selected, and projected financial statements.

## Common deficiencies that delay registration
1. NOCs of the concerned State RCS not obtained — required under section 22 for conversion proposals, or rule 3(1)(g) of the MSCS Rules, 2002 for new credit and multipurpose societies
2. Unsigned membership list, or missing ID proofs
3. Bank certificate opened in the Chief Promoter's name instead of the society's, or showing a zero balance
4. Society name containing 'Bhartiya', 'Indian', 'India' or 'National', contrary to rule 3(1)(h)
5. Amended MSCS Act and Rules not fully incorporated into the proposed bye-laws
6. Key details in the proposal not matching the bye-laws (authorised share capital, dividend rate, minimum level of products or services)
7. Interim board committee details not matching the proposed board composition
8. Missing society seal on the final application

## Ingestion notes

Item 5 of the deficiencies list is the tell that the model must never rely on
an unamended bye-law template: CRCS itself rejects proposals whose bye-laws
predate the 2023 amendments. Answers about bye-law content should always be
qualified by whether the society's bye-laws have been aligned post-2023.
